🎨 POPES framework for political cartoon analysis

P

People

Who appears or is referenced in this cartoon?

  • Famous or specifically labeled individuals (President, celebrity)
  • Specific groups or organizations (NRA, NAACP, Congress)
  • A mix of individuals meant to represent larger populations (voters, college students, sports fans, etc.)
O

Objects and symbols

What appears in this cartoon? What are they representing?

  • Known symbols (elephant, donkey, Uncle Sam)
  • Labeled objects (ship with Articles of Confederation labeled)
  • Unlabeled objects
P

Parallels

What connections are drawn between two unrelated events?

  • A current event and a historical event
  • A current event and pop culture
  • Two current events
  • Side by side comparison
  • Not all cartoons will make a parallel
E

Emotions

What are the various people's or symbols' feelings in the cartoon?

  • Positive: anticipation, content, eager, grateful, luck, relief, confidence
  • Negative: dread, hurt, disappointment, panic, embarrassment, outrage
  • Unsure: oblivious, shocked, uncertain
S

Synthesis

How can you summarize all these pieces in a concise sentence?

  • What is the cartoonist doing? (celebrating, questioning, criticizing, etc.)
  • How does he/she do it? (people, objects & symbols used, parallels made, emotions)

The five topics

📜 Articles of Confederation

What you need to know

  • Powers of Congress: What could and couldn't the federal government do?
  • Strengths: What worked well under the Articles?
  • Weaknesses: What problems led to the Constitutional Convention?

Study materials

  • 📋 Articles of Confederation simulation
  • 🎥 A More Perfect Union video guide

🏛️ Constitutional Convention

What you need to know

  • Plans proposed: Virginia Plan, New Jersey Plan, Great Compromise
  • Location: Where the Convention was held
  • Outcome: What was created and how compromises were reached

Study materials

  • 🎥 A More Perfect Union video guide
  • 📖 Constitutional Convention book reading

📋 Preamble to the Constitution

What you need to know

  • Goals of government: Describe each of the 6 goals
  • Application: Apply these goals to real-world scenarios
  • Key phrases: Know what each phrase means

Study materials

  • 📝 Note sheet in your note packet
  • 🔄 Sorting activity in your note packet

⚖️ Principles of the Constitution

What you need to know

  • Definitions: Know what each principle means
  • Examples: Identify examples of each principle in action
  • All 8 principles: popular sovereignty, limited government, separation of powers, checks and balances, federalism, republicanism, individual rights, rule of law

Study materials

  • 📝 Note sheet in your note packet
  • 💻 Notes posted in the Week 7 module of Canvas

📋 Bill of Rights

What you need to know

  • Rights guaranteed: Know what each of the first 10 amendments protects
  • Real-world application: Identify which amendment applies to different scenarios
  • Key freedoms: Speech, religion, press, assembly, petition, and more

Study materials

  • 🎯 You've Got Rights activity
  • 📝 Notes from slideshow
  • 💻 Week 9 module of Canvas

💡 Study tips for success

📚 Review strategy

  • Use the flashcards to memorize key terms
  • Take the practice quiz multiple times
  • Play the Constitutional Cases game for Bill of Rights practice
  • Use Study Recommendations to identify weak areas

🎯 Focus areas

  • Practice the POPES framework with political cartoons
  • Review your note packets thoroughly
  • Make sure you can apply concepts to new scenarios
  • Know specific examples for each principle and amendment
